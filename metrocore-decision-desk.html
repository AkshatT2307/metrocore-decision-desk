CARE 2026  /  Data Challenge

MetroCore Decision Desk

Build a system that reads a city department's memo, with attached images, and returns the mathematically optimal answer.

Input text + images Output one number, or na Models open weights, up to 7B Network none
The challenge

Optimization problems, described in plain language

MetroCore is a metropolitan area of 4.2 million people. Its various city departments face operational decision problems every day: scheduling shifts, routing vehicles, allocating budgets, planning infrastructure. You will receive optimization problems written the way real people write, as word problems, memos, and policy excerpts.

Your mission: build a system that takes a problem description (text and, optionally, images) and returns the mathematically optimal answer.

What your system receives

A word problem in a department head's voice, plus images that carry the data

Each problem consists of text and, optionally, one or more images. Images, when present, carry data that is essential to solving the problem: tables, charts, maps, or scanned documents. All numerical data, constraints, and objectives are spread across the text and images together.

Expect city-staff writing

  • Figures that do not matter, mixed in with ones that do.
  • Mixed units and conventions: tons and kg, weekly and monthly, lakh and crore.
  • Rules any competent analyst would assume without writing them down, such as "a nurse cannot work two wards at once".
  • Key data supplied only inside an attached image, not repeated in the text.

Example problem types

Type A · image holds a table

Transit and logistics

"Assign buses to the six new routes starting Monday. Minimize total deadhead kilometres. The distance matrix is attached."

A photographed table of pairwise distances between depots and route start-points.

Type B · image holds a chart

Energy grid management

"Attached is the hourly demand forecast for next week. Schedule our three generators to meet demand at minimum fuel cost."

Demand is a bar chart in the image. Generator capacities and per-MWh costs are in the text.

Type C · image holds a map

Parks, environment, public services

"The map shows eight candidate locations and the river segments each covers. Place the fewest stations so every segment is monitored."

The river network and candidate sites exist only in the attached image.

What your system returns

One value per problem

numberThe optimal objective value, at full floating-point precision.
naNo feasible solution exists, because the constraints contradict each other.

The expected output is the optimal objective value only. No variable assignments, explanations, or intermediate work are required.

Scope and scale

A broad range of optimization domains, more than 60 problem families

Problems cover logistics, scheduling, resource allocation, infrastructure planning, financial optimization, and more. Each problem is a self-contained optimization task described in natural language.

< 10 decisions

Scoring

Be right, or say na when it is infeasible

Let A be your answer and A* the reference optimal value.

Your answerScore
Correct: |A − A*| / max(1, |A*|) ≤ 0.01+1
Close: within 5% of A*+0.5
Wrong number (feasible problem, off by more than 5%)0
Wrong number on an infeasible problem (should be na)−0.5
na on a feasible problem−0.5
Correct na on an infeasible problem+1
Missing answer or crash0
Final score = mean of per-problem scores across the evaluation set

Crashes

Problems answered before a crash keep their scores. Unanswered problems score 0.

Models

Open-weight models of up to 7B parameters. Multiple models may run in sequence. Quantization and fine-tuning are permitted on any data participants have the right to use.

Libraries

ENVIRONMENT.md lists every preinstalled package, and all teams receive the same environment. Additional open-source packages may be bundled. Commercial software and anything requiring a license server are prohibited.

Output

Your notebook writes submission.csv with two columns: problem_id and answer.

Rules

Keep it offline, general, and reproducible

  1. No internet

    No calls to any external model or service.

  2. No hardcoding

    The pipeline must work on problems it has never seen. Top-scoring teams may be rerun on a renumbered, paraphrased copy of the Dev set. A significant score drop faces review and possible disqualification.

  3. No tampering

    Do not attack the harness or attempt to read hidden data. Violations lead to disqualification.

  4. Reproduction

    Prize winners hand over their code and reproduce their score on organizer hardware. Failure to reproduce forfeits the prize to the next-ranked team.

Technical report

At most 2,500 words, excluding references and appendices

Architecture overview

A clear system diagram and end-to-end description of the pipeline, from raw text and images to the final number. Describe every major component and how they interact.

Novelty statement

What is new or non-obvious: a prompting strategy, a formulation-extraction step, an unconventional solver integration, a specialized vision pipeline, or anything that separates you from a naïve baseline.

Model details

For every model: name and size, quantization scheme, fine-tuning data and procedure, and its role in the pipeline. If models are composed, explain the routing or sequencing logic.

Image-understanding pipeline

How tables, charts, and maps become structured data: preprocessing, extraction, and any verification steps.

The jury rewards depth of insight, reproducibility, and clarity over polish.
